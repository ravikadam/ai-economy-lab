Implementation Team Challenge · how the game runs

AI Economy Lab

Your team runs an AI implementation firm for up to 120 simulated months. You pick enterprise use cases, choose models and budgets, and run pilots, launches and scale-ups. Rival firms are reinforcement-learning agents trained on this same economy on an M2 Pro. The trained policy also coaches your team on every decision.

The economy your firm lives in

Every firm sits in the same value chain. Products flow toward enterprises and money flows back up. Demand also travels backward: when your customers use a product, that load reaches all the way to the chip fab, where new capacity takes 18 months to arrive.

One month, in order

Each turn is one simulated month. All firms decide at the same time, without seeing each other's current moves. The engine then resolves the month in a fixed order, so the same seed and the same decisions always reproduce the same world.

  1. Shocks and arrivals. Fab shortages (3–9 months), model releases every 6–18 months, chips and capacity that were ordered earlier.
  2. Publish the market. Prices, capacity, confidence and launches are published with a one-month lag.
  3. Collect decisions. One decision per firm: your team, RL rivals and rule-based firms all submit simultaneously.
  4. Reserve budgets. Spend is checked against cash, credit and your reserve floor. Contested pilot customers are allocated fairly. An illegal move becomes a no-op with a penalty.
  5. Advance projects. Development progresses, and stage gates sample success with a seeded random draw.
  6. Demand. Enterprises adopt or churn, inference runs, and compute is shared out proportionally if it is short.
  7. Settle. Overhead, support, interest and credit draws. A firm that cannot pay goes bankrupt and its customers leave.
  8. Update the market. Compute and chip prices, enterprise confidence, supplier investment and new entrants.
  9. Score. Change in firm value is your raw score; the risk-adjusted score also reflects your risk appetite.

A use case from idea to scale

Every project passes two independent gates. The technical gate asks whether the model can do the task. The commercial gate asks whether enterprises get more value than they pay and whether the model meets their reliability requirement. A product can pass the first gate and fail the second.

p(technical) = sigmoid( 6·(model capability − difficulty) + skill + R&D effort − 1.5·integration burden + 0.4 ), clamped to [0.03, 0.97]

You never see true difficulty, only a noisy signal that sharpens after research and after each attempt. Model releases raise capability, so a use case that is infeasible today can open up later.

Two learning loops, one simulator

The machine learns by playing thousands of episodes. Your team learns by playing one, with the trained policy as both opponent and coach. The same engine and the same legality rules drive both loops.

How a team is scored

Value created

Change in firm value in $M: cash minus debt, plus the present value of live products and a stage-weighted book value of projects still in development. Bankruptcy wipes it out.

Risk-adjusted score

Value created, minus penalties for losses, thin runway and debt, weighted by the risk appetite you chose: cautious, balanced or aggressive.

Rank and survival

Your place among every firm that lived in the market, including entrants and firms that went bankrupt.

Coach gap

After the game, the coach replays your seed from month 0 in your seat. The difference shows how much value your decisions left on the table, or won.

Lessons the coach flags

Every decision is checked against an implementation playbook, using only information you had when you made it.

Running it

streamlit run viewer/app.py                                   # play, replay, dashboards
python -m training.train --config config/train.yaml          # BC warm start + PPO (MPS)
python -m training.evaluate --checkpoint runs/latest --seeds 100:129 --scenarios "config/scenarios/*.yaml" --ablations
python -m scripts.simulate --config config/base.yaml --seed 42 --plots
python -m scripts.benchmark                                  # CPU vs MPS, throughput

This is a stylised simulator. Every parameter is an illustrative, configurable default, not a forecast of real AI markets or vendor economics. It is built to rehearse implementation judgement under uncertainty, not to predict outcomes.
